## 1. BLOCKER — Phase F's "hLG closes by construction" contradicts its own key lemma (Ruling Change 2 + Phase F.1/F.5)

Change 2 claims locks become resolution-free, so declared- and actual-side locks are *syntactically identical* and hLG (verified: Preservation.lean:1345-1351 — `CapyTy.compile T2 ctxLockA` vs `ctxLockAct`, same source type, two compiler contexts differing in a stored type) dies by construction. But F.1's **overlap-aware pairing** requires manufacture to decide which entries may be pair-claimed using **peak-disjointness at manufacture time**, and peaks resolve term vars through *stored types* (`peaksVarBound`, Capybara/Syntax/Context.lean:223-236) — exactly what narrowing changes. Since `SepCtx` is a flat list claiming **all** pairs (`HasTwoDistinct` = any two distinct entries; SepCtx.lean:8), selective pairing must materialize as entry grouping/merging — a manufacture *output*. The roadmap's stability argument ("narrowing only shrinks resolutions; disjointness anti-monotone") shows claimed pairs stay *true* but concedes the test's truth value can flip overlap→disjoint under narrowing (`T0y <: T0` whose captureSet drops the shared atom from `ann(y)`). Then declared-context manufacture groups `{y},{c}` while actual-context manufacture pairs them: **syntactically different locks — hLG resurrected**. The resolution-free alternative (syntactic-overlap grouping) fails the roadmap's own example (`{y}` vs `{c}` with `c ∈ ann(y)` are syntactically disjoint), making the wrap unsatisfiable for a harmless program. **Amendment:** before Phase F, write down where pairing metadata lives and the invariance device (e.g., grouping always computed in the *declared* context threaded through, G-style, or grouping carried in the compiled type so both sides agree). Do not execute F on the "by construction" promise as written.
